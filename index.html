Welcome to Anatomy of AI

A familiar comparison, not an equivalence. This class is not claiming that AI is like humans, conscious, alive, or biologically equivalent. We use the intelligence system as it exists in humans as a familiar comparison point for understanding the operating structure of artificial intelligence.

What we will map: sensing and input, processing and model, context and memory, feedback, outputs and actions, and guardrails. The comparison helps us name system roles and information flow without overclaiming what AI is.

03 · Prompt & context

From prompt to working context

A prompt is what you ask. Context is what the system needs around the ask to do the work responsibly.

Bare request → Add verified context → Add operating rules → Reviewable result

When information is missing, do not guess. Mark what is unknown, then ask for the evidence, decision, owner, or approval needed next.

PASS 1

Bare request

What we give it
The task, without the background.
What improves
It knows the ask, but the answer may be generic.

Example“Draft three content ideas for NUGES.”

PASS 2

Add verified context

What we give it
The ask + verified facts, audience, examples, and constraints.
What improves
The draft can fit NUGES and the people we want to reach.

Example“Use the verified NUGES brief, its audience, and approved examples.”

PASS 3

Add operating rules

What we give it
Source rules, uncertainty handling, human approval, and output format.
What improves
Claims can be checked; unknowns and approval needs stay visible.

Example“Show sources and gaps in a table. Draft only; wait for human approval.”

RESULT

Reviewable result

What we give it
The ask + known facts + working rules.
What improves
A more useful, safer output for a human to review, not automatic truth.

Example output“Idea: explain a service once verified. Source required: service evidence. Status: draft, not approved.”

For every NUGES pass: Do not invent facts. Draft only. Do not publish or act externally without explicit human approval.

Tutor detail: complete working prompt & source rules

Prompt engineering shapes the ask. Context engineering supplies and maintains the facts, examples, constraints, and permissions around it. Add each pass to the previous one; context is not automatically true.

Using the shared NUGES Media organizational context, draft three low-risk content ideas for the audience specified in the supplied brief. Use supplied verified public facts, approved public sources, or explicitly authorized internal statements within the allowed data boundary. Use approved examples and the brief's constraints. If these inputs are missing, ask for them.

Do not invent services, clients, results, prices, staff, or claims. Show the source basis for each idea. Separate verified or authorized information from proposals; do not treat a proposal as fact.

For every missing or uncertain item, mark Source required, Decision required, Owner required, or Approval required. Record the evidence, decision, person or role, or explicit approval needed and the next action. Never infer facts, permissions, or approval owners.

Output a table: idea | audience fit | source basis | unknowns and next action | review status.

Draft only. Do not publish or act externally without explicit human approval. If the reviewer or approver is unknown, mark Owner required and ask who decides.
04 · Practical collaborative build
SHARED NUGES CONTEXT

Build this together

The room decides what the context engine needs before anyone treats it as fact.

Biology analogy: the heart and vessels circulate what the body needs. AI mapping: shared context distributes what the task needs.

Test one real task, with low risk. Define its objective, audience, inputs, data boundary, tools, reviewer, approval rule, and definition of done. Then test the context engine.

Anchor prompt

We are co-building a shared NUGES organizational context engine. Use verified public sources or authorized statements. Label every item Verified public, Authorized internal, Proposed, Source required, Decision required, Owner required, or Approval required. For every unresolved item, record the next action. Never infer facts, permissions, or approval owners.

Room prompt

Ask the room to choose one real, low-risk NUGES task. Record audience, objective, allowed data, prohibited data, reviewer, approval gate, and definition of done. For every unknown field, write the responsible next action: Source required, Decision required, Owner required, or Approval required.
05 · Assignment

Build your own personal context engine

Hand off from the collective body to your own context engine.

  1. Choose one recurring task.
  2. Identify the context it needs.
  3. Define one review or approval boundary.

Personal sequence

Interview me about my role, responsibilities, goals, recurring tasks, audiences, preferences, outputs, quality rules, privacy boundaries, decision rules, and one low-risk test task. Ask, do not guess. Do not guess organization-wide details. Classify each unresolved item as Source required, Decision required, Owner required, or Approval required, and record the next action. Assemble my personal context engine v1 and ask me to correct anything untrue before saving.
